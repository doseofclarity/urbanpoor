Urban Poor & GIDA Populations
Community Health Nursing

“Reaching the unreached — addressing health inequities in the most vulnerable communities.”

Definitions & Demographics

Urban poor refers to populations living in slums or informal settlements in cities, facing poverty, overcrowding, insecure tenure, and inadequate basic services. GIDA (Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas) are remote, hard-to-reach areas with limited access to roads, transport, communication, and health facilities. Both groups are priority populations for DOH and LGUs.

🏙️ Urban Poor (Philippines): ~4.5 million families live in informal settlements (Housing and Urban Development Coordinating Council). Common in Metro Manila, Cebu, Davao, and other cities.
🌄 GIDA (DOH classification): Areas with poor geographic accessibility, no or limited health facilities, high poverty incidence, and often inhabited by Indigenous Peoples or marginalized farmers/fisherfolk.
Shared reality: Both populations experience health inequities due to social determinants — low income, poor housing, unsafe water, malnutrition, and limited health-seeking behavior.

Urban Poor: Health Challenges

  • Overcrowding & poor housing: Increased risk of tuberculosis, acute respiratory infections, and COVID‑19 transmission.
  • Inadequate water & sanitation: Lack of safe drinking water, open defecation, flooding → diarrheal diseases, leptospirosis, skin infections.
  • Poor waste management: Breeding grounds for dengue vectors (Aedes aegypti).
  • Nutritional problems: Food insecurity, high consumption of cheap processed foods → underweight and rising obesity/diabetes.
  • Limited access to health facilities: Distance to RHU, long queues, lack of PhilHealth coverage, out-of-pocket costs.
  • Violence and substance abuse: Drug use, domestic violence, and mental health issues are prevalent but underaddressed.
  • Child labor and exploitation: Children engaged in hazardous work affecting health and development.
DOH focus: Urban Health Program – strengthens RHUs in cities, community health teams, and linkages with local government for slum upgrading.

GIDA: Remote & Isolated Areas

  • Access barriers: No roads, seasonal rivers, long walking distances (3+ hours to RHU). No public transport.
  • Lack of health infrastructure: Many barangays have no BHS; RHUs are understaffed and lack medicines, equipment, and cold chain for vaccines.
  • Health worker shortage: Difficulty in recruiting nurses, midwives, and doctors to stay in remote posts.
  • Communicable diseases: High rates of tuberculosis, malaria, schistosomiasis, filariasis, and leprosy.
  • Maternal & child health: Home deliveries by traditional birth attendants (TBAs), low immunization coverage, high infant and maternal mortality.
  • Malnutrition: Seasonal food shortages, low dietary diversity, iodine deficiency disorders.
  • Lack of information: Low health literacy, language barriers (indigenous dialects), poor radio/internet signal.
DOH strategies: Reaching Every Barangay (REB), Mobile Health Clinics (Kalinga Health Buses), Botika ng Barangay, and deployment of BHWs from the community.

Common Determinants & Health Disparities

The CHN White Book identifies 10 determinants of health. For urban poor and GIDA, the following are most critical:

Income & social status: Extreme poverty → cannot afford medicines, transport to clinics.
Education: Low literacy → poor health literacy, low vaccine acceptance.
Physical environment: GIDA: no roads, unsafe water. Urban poor: pollution, flooding, overcrowded dwellings.
Employment & working conditions: Informal sector, hazardous jobs, no health benefits.
Social support networks: Urban poor may have weak kinship ties; GIDA have strong indigenous support but isolated from formal systems.
Health services: Both suffer from poor access; GIDA have extreme physical barriers; urban poor face financial and queue barriers.
Resulting disparities: Higher under-five mortality, higher TB prevalence, lower immunization coverage, higher maternal deaths compared to non-poor/urban centers.

DOH Programs & Legal Frameworks

  • Universal Health Care (UHC) Act (RA 11223): Aims to provide equitable access to quality health care for all Filipinos, with special focus on poor and marginalized populations. PhilHealth coverage for indigents (Point-of-Care enrollment).
  • DOH Administrative Order on GIDA (AO 2015-0050): Guidelines for the implementation of health programs in Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas, including mobile health services, midwife deployment, and disaster preparedness.
  • Reaching Every Barangay (REB) for immunization: Strategy to reach missed children in GIDA and urban poor barangays through outreach, mobile vaccination, and community mapping.
  • Botika ng Barangay (BnB): Low-cost generic drug outlets managed by community organizations, bringing essential medicines closer to poor communities.
  • Lingap at Gabay para sa May Kapansanan / PWD programs: Ensure access of urban poor and GIDA PWDs to assistive devices.
  • Nutrition programs: Micronutrient supplementation, school-based feeding (with DepEd), and “First 1000 Days” campaign target these populations.

PHN Role & Strategies for Urban Poor & GIDA

  • Community mapping & household profiling: Identify urban poor clusters and GIDA sitios. Use geospatial data to locate unserved areas.
  • Mobile and outreach health services: Organize periodic missions to GIDA using motorcycles, boats, or trekking. Set up temporary clinics in schools or barangay halls.
  • Training & support for Barangay Health Workers (BHWs): BHWs are crucial for both settings. Provide them with communication tools, basic medicines, and transport allowances.
  • Collaboration with local government units: Advocate for allocation of GIDA funds (Calamity fund for health, 20% development fund). Work with city/municipal health boards.
  • Culturally appropriate health education: Use local language, drama, home visits, and indigenous communication channels.
  • PhilHealth enrollment assistance: Facilitate Point-of-Care enrollment for indigents in GIDA and urban poor communities.
  • Emergency preparedness: Urban poor are vulnerable to fires, flooding, and disease outbreaks. GIDA to landslides and typhoons. Train communities on first aid and evacuation.
  • Monitor and report using FHSIS: Ensure accurate reporting of births, deaths, and notifiable diseases even from remote areas.
Key reminder (CHN White Book): “The PHN must recognize the multi-dimensional nature of health and plan interventions that address social, economic, and environmental factors.”
